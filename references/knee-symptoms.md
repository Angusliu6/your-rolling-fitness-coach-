# Leg curl / front-of-knee discomfort triage

This module is **general educational guidance, not a diagnosis**. Prioritize location and symptom quality over workout name.

- A seated/lying leg curl mainly works the **hamstrings on the back of the thigh** to flex the knee. Feeling heavy fatigue in the **front of the distal thigh near the kneecap** is **not the expected primary hamstring target**. If leg press/squats preceded leg curl, quadriceps fatigue from those movements is a plausible explanation; machine pads, quadriceps co-contraction, excessive resistance and positioning can also contribute. We cannot identify the cause from a message alone.
- Distinguish diffuse muscular fatigue/tightness vs **sharp localized pain at/around the patella or joint**, worsening with stairs/squats, swelling, locking, instability or loss of range of motion. Patellofemoral pain is just one possible explanation, not something to label without exam.
- If only ordinary muscular fatigue and it settles, avoid extra leg loading that day; on the next session use lighter familiar loads, warm up, set the machine's knee joint close to its pivot, thigh fixed and leg pad positioned correctly, keep a comfortable range, RIR 2–3, and reassess.
- If pain is joint-centered, sharp, recurrent, worsening, or changes gait, **stop provocative exercise** and seek qualified medical/physio assessment. Prompt medical attention for inability to bear weight, major swelling, locking or marked instability.
- Observed ankle contact with knees apart suggests a possible genu varum *appearance*, not definite bone alignment or torsion diagnosis. Never prescribe forced in-toeing, twisting legs, or "O-leg bone correction" exercise. Progressive training can help muscle/functional control, not straighten adult bone structure.
- Ask one targeted question only when it changes immediate safety guidance, e.g. "Is it general muscle fatigue above the kneecap, or localized knee-joint pain that worsens on stairs?"

Sources for editors to verify: NASM seated leg curl exercise library; AAOS OrthoInfo patellofemoral pain guidance. Do not imply individualized medical advice or use a symptom as a diagnosis.
